Infrastructure Philosophy

// On Understanding Systems

"Know the why before the how" isn't just a catchphrase and it has saved me countless hours of fixing the wrong problem. Every system has a story, every architecture decision has context. Take the time to understand both.

// On Automation

Automate everything you do more than twice, but understand what you're automating first. The best automation comes from deep understanding of manual processes. Also, always include a manual override. Future you will thank present you.

// On Learning

The homelab isn't just about playing with tech. Your homelab is about having a safe space to fail spectacularly. Every outage is a lesson, every recovery is growth. Document everything, especially the failures.

// On Digital Rights

Infrastructure should be transparent, repairable, and under your control. Whether it's the right to repair hardware or the ability to audit code, ownership means actual ownership. This extends to data sovereignty it is your data, your rules.

How I Write Things Down

At review time, an undocumented contribution and a contribution that didn't happen look identical. Same goes at 3 AM when the person on call isn't you.

// Record the command, not the number

A runbook that says "there are 17 zones" is wrong the moment somebody adds one, and it is wrong silently, because the reader has no way to know. A runbook that says "run this to list the zones" stays correct. Volatile values don't belong in documentation; the way to obtain them does.

// Mark every claim confirmed or unverified

Half of what ends up in a runbook is observed and half is inferred, and once it's all in the same font the reader can't tell which is which. So each claim carries its status explicitly. An honest UNVERIFIED tells the next person where to start checking, and it stops my assumptions from calcifying into somebody else's facts.

// Never transcribe a secret

Documentation gets copied, synced, pasted into chat and handed to new starters. Nothing that would need rotating if it leaked goes in it. The doc records where a credential lives and how to retrieve it, never its value. No exceptions, because the exceptions are what you find in an old note two years later.

// Cross-link by dependency, not by folder

Folder structure describes how the author filed something. Dependency links describe what will break if you change it. During an incident only the second is useful, so runbooks link to what they depend on and what depends on them.
